# 📖 Introduction

Every business performs financial transactions every day.

Examples include:

- Purchasing goods
- Selling products
- Paying salaries
- Receiving payments
- Paying rent
- Depositing money in the bank

These transactions must first be **recorded**, and later **analyzed** to understand the financial performance of the business.

This is where **bookkeeping** and **accounting** work together.

---

# 📒 What is Bookkeeping?

**Bookkeeping** is the process of recording day-to-day financial transactions in a systematic manner.

It focuses on maintaining accurate and complete records.

Typical bookkeeping activities include:

- Recording sales
- Recording purchases
- Recording receipts
- Recording payments
- Maintaining cash books
- Maintaining journals
- Maintaining ledgers

Bookkeeping answers the question:

> **"What transactions occurred?"**

---

# 📘 What is Accounting?

Accounting is a broader process.

It begins where bookkeeping ends.

Accounting includes:

- Recording transactions
- Classifying accounts
- Summarizing information
- Preparing financial statements
- Analyzing financial performance
- Interpreting financial results
- Communicating information to stakeholders

Accounting answers questions such as:

- Is the business making a profit?
- What is the financial position?
- Should the business invest more?
- How can performance improve?

---

# ⚖️ Bookkeeping vs Accounting

| Feature | Bookkeeping | Accounting |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Meaning | Recording financial transactions | Recording, analyzing, interpreting, and reporting financial information |
| Objective | Maintain records | Provide useful financial information |
| Scope | Narrow | Broad |
| Stage | First step | Next step after bookkeeping |
| Skill Required | Basic accounting knowledge | Advanced accounting knowledge |
| Decision Making | Does not support major decisions | Supports business decisions |
| Financial Statements | Not prepared | Prepared |
| Analysis | Not performed | Performed |
| Users | Bookkeepers | Accountants, Managers, Investors |

---

# 🔗 Relationship Between Bookkeeping and Accounting

Bookkeeping and accounting are closely related.

```text
Business Transactions
          │
          ▼
     Bookkeeping
 (Recording Transactions)
          │
          ▼
      Accounting
(Classify • Summarize • Analyze • Interpret)
          │
          ▼
 Financial Statements
          │
          ▼
 Better Business Decisions
```

Without bookkeeping, accounting cannot begin.

Without accounting, bookkeeping records cannot be fully utilized for decision-making.

---

# 🌟 Importance of Both

Both bookkeeping and accounting help businesses:

- Maintain organized financial records.
- Monitor income and expenses.
- Measure profits and losses.
- Prepare financial statements.
- Comply with legal requirements.
- Support business planning.
- Build investor confidence.
- Improve financial decision-making.

---

# 🏪 Real-Life Example

Suppose a stationery shop performs these transactions:

- Purchased notebooks.
- Sold pens to customers.
- Paid electricity bills.
- Received payment from customers.

### Bookkeeper's Role

The bookkeeper records every transaction in the books of accounts.

### Accountant's Role

The accountant uses those records to prepare:

- Trading Account
- Profit & Loss Account
- Balance Sheet

The accountant then analyzes whether the business earned a profit and suggests improvements.

---

# ✅ Advantages

## Bookkeeping

- Maintains systematic records.
- Reduces errors.
- Simplifies accounting work.
- Keeps financial data organized.

### Accounting

- Helps management make decisions.
- Determines profit or loss.
- Shows financial position.
- Assists investors and banks.
- Supports budgeting and planning.

---

# ⚠️ Limitations

## Bookkeeping

- Does not analyze financial information.
- Cannot determine business performance.
- Limited to recording transactions.

### Accounting

- Depends on accurate bookkeeping.
- Requires professional knowledge.
- Uses estimates in some situations.
- Historical information may not always predict future performance.

---

# 📝 Summary

Bookkeeping and accounting are different but complementary.

Bookkeeping focuses on recording financial transactions.

Accounting uses those records to prepare reports, analyze financial performance, and support decision-making.

Both are essential for every business.

# 🔑 Key Takeaways

- Bookkeeping is the systematic recording of financial transactions.
- Accounting is the process of recording, classifying, summarizing, analyzing, interpreting, and communicating financial information.
- Bookkeeping is the foundation of accounting.
- Accounting provides meaningful financial reports for decision-making.
- Every successful business requires both bookkeeping and accounting.
