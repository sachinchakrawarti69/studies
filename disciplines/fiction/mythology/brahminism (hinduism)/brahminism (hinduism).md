# Hinduism (Historically: Brahmanism)

## Overview

**Hinduism** is one of the world's oldest living religions and a diverse family of religious, philosophical, and cultural traditions that originated in the Indian subcontinent. It has no single founder, central authority, or universally accepted creed. Hinduism encompasses a wide range of beliefs, practices, schools of philosophy, and devotional traditions.

The term **Brahmanism** is primarily used by historians to describe the Vedic religious tradition centered on the authority of the Brahmin priesthood before and during the early development of classical Hinduism. Modern scholars generally distinguish Brahmanism from contemporary Hinduism, although the two are historically connected.

# Etymology

The word **Hindu** derives from the Sanskrit **Sindhu** (the Indus River). Persian speakers pronounced "S" as "H," leading to "Hindu." The term originally referred to the people living beyond the Indus River before becoming associated with religious traditions.

---

# Historical Development

## Vedic Period

The earliest religious traditions are preserved in the **Vedas**, the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism.

Features:

- Fire sacrifices (Yajna)
- Vedic hymns
- Priestly rituals
- Worship of natural and cosmic deities

---

## Brahmanism

Brahmanism emphasized:

- Authority of Brahmin priests
- Vedic rituals
- Sacrificial ceremonies
- Social and religious duties

It gradually evolved alongside philosophical developments that gave rise to many ideas central to Hinduism.

---

## Classical Hinduism

Later developments introduced:

- Temple worship
- Bhakti (devotion)
- Yoga
- Philosophical schools
- Epics and Puranic traditions
- Worship of Vishnu, Shiva, Devi, and many other deities

---

# Core Concepts

## Brahman

The ultimate, eternal reality underlying all existence.

---

## Atman

The eternal self or soul.

Many Hindu philosophical schools explore the relationship between Atman and Brahman.

---

## Dharma

Righteous duty, moral order, and ethical living.

---

## Karma

The principle that actions have consequences affecting this and future lives.

---

## Samsara

The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth.

---

## Moksha

Liberation from Samsara and realization of ultimate truth.

# Summary

Hinduism is a diverse and ancient religious tradition rooted in the Vedic heritage of the Indian subcontinent. It encompasses a broad range of philosophies, devotional practices, rituals, and cultural traditions centered on concepts such as **Dharma**, **Karma**, **Samsara**, and **Moksha**. While **Brahmanism** refers to an important historical phase emphasizing Vedic ritual and priestly traditions, modern Hinduism has evolved into a rich and multifaceted religion practiced by over a billion people worldwide.
