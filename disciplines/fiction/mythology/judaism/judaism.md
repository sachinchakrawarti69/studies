# Judaism

## Overview

**Judaism** is one of the world's oldest monotheistic religions and the oldest of the Abrahamic religions. It originated in the ancient Near East and is centered on the covenant between God and the people of Israel. Judaism emphasizes ethical living, worship of one God, study of sacred texts, and observance of religious laws and traditions.

Followers of Judaism are called **Jews**. Judaism is both a religion and, for many people, an ethnic and cultural identity.

# Etymology

The word **Judaism** comes from **Judah**, one of the twelve tribes of Israel and later the Kingdom of Judah.

The Hebrew term for Judaism is **Yahadut (יהדות)**.

---

# Historical Background

## Patriarchal Period

According to Jewish tradition:

- Abraham entered into a covenant with God.
- Isaac and Jacob continued this covenant.
- Jacob's descendants became the Twelve Tribes of Israel.

---

## Exodus

According to the Hebrew Bible:

- Moses led the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt.
- God gave the Torah to Moses at Mount Sinai.
- The covenant between God and Israel was reaffirmed.

---

## Kingdoms of Israel and Judah

The Israelites established kingdoms ruled by kings including:

- Saul
- David
- Solomon

The First Temple in Jerusalem was traditionally built during Solomon's reign.

---

## Exile and Return

- The First Temple was destroyed by the Babylonians in 586 BCE.
- Many Jews were exiled to Babylon.
- The Second Temple was later built after the return from exile.

---

## Second Temple Period

This period saw the development of many Jewish religious traditions.

The Second Temple was destroyed by the Romans in 70 CE, leading to the growth of Rabbinic Judaism.

---

## Rabbinic Judaism

Following the destruction of the Second Temple:

- Synagogues became central places of worship.
- Rabbis became leading religious teachers.
- Study of the Torah and Talmud became increasingly important.

- Ethical living

# God

Judaism teaches belief in one eternal God who is:

- Creator of the universe
- Omnipotent
- Omniscient
- Just
- Merciful

The personal name of God represented by the Hebrew letters **YHWH** is treated with great reverence and is generally not pronounced in religious practice.

# Sacred Scriptures

## Tanakh

The Hebrew Bible consists of three sections:

### Torah

The Five Books of Moses:

- Genesis
- Exodus
- Leviticus
- Numbers
- Deuteronomy

---

### Nevi'im

The Prophets.

---

### Ketuvim

The Writings.

---

## Talmud

The Talmud contains centuries of rabbinic discussions on Jewish law, ethics, customs, and interpretation of the Torah.

It consists primarily of:

- Mishnah
- Gemara

---

# Commandments

The Torah traditionally contains **613 mitzvot (commandments)** covering religious, ethical, and social life.

Examples include:

- Honoring parents
- Observing the Sabbath
- Charity
- Justice
- Care for the poor
- Dietary laws (Kashrut)

---

# Worship

Jewish worship commonly includes:

- Prayer
- Torah reading
- Study
- Community worship
- Festivals
- Acts of charity (Tzedakah)

---

# Sabbath (Shabbat)

Shabbat begins at sunset on Friday and ends after sunset on Saturday.

It is observed as:

- Day of rest
- Prayer
- Family gathering
- Celebration
- Spiritual reflection

---

# Major Festivals

Commemorates the Exodus from Egypt.

---

## Rosh Hashanah

---

## Yom Kippur

Day of Atonement.

Considered the holiest day of the Jewish year.

---

## Sukkot

Festival commemorating the Israelites' journey in the wilderness.

---

## Hanukkah

Festival of Lights.

Celebrates the rededication of the Second Temple.

---

## Purim

Celebrates the events recorded in the Book of Esther.

---

## Shavuot

Commemorates the giving of the Torah at Mount Sinai.

---

# Dietary Laws (Kashrut)

Foods that comply with Jewish dietary law are called **Kosher**.

Examples include:

- Separation of meat and dairy
- Only certain animals are permitted
- Specific methods of food preparation

Observance varies among Jewish communities.

---

# Major Branches

## Orthodox Judaism

Places strong emphasis on traditional Jewish law and practice.

---

## Conservative Judaism

Seeks to preserve tradition while allowing for measured development in interpretation.

---

## Reform Judaism

Emphasizes the evolving nature of Jewish practice and individual autonomy.

---

## Reconstructionist Judaism

Views Judaism as an evolving religious civilization.

---

## Humanistic Judaism

Focuses on Jewish culture and identity from a non-theistic perspective.

# Summary

Judaism is an ancient monotheistic religion centered on the covenant between God and the people of Israel. It emphasizes the study of the Torah, ethical conduct, community life, and the observance of religious traditions. Over thousands of years, Judaism has developed diverse religious movements while maintaining its foundational commitment to monotheism, justice, learning, and the enduring significance of the covenant.
