# Islam

## Overview

**Islam** is a monotheistic Abrahamic religion founded in the 7th century CE in the Arabian Peninsula. Muslims believe that God (**Allah**) revealed His final message to the Prophet Muhammad through the angel Jibril (Gabriel). Islam teaches belief in one God, righteous living, compassion, justice, and accountability in the afterlife.

Today, Islam is one of the world's largest religions, with over 1.9 billion followers across every continent.

# Etymology

The word **Islam** comes from the Arabic root **S-L-M**, associated with peace, safety, and submission. A Muslim is "one who submits to God."

---

# Core Beliefs

Islam is founded on belief in:

- One God (Allah)
- Angels
- Revealed scriptures
- Prophets and messengers
- Day of Judgment
- Divine decree (Qadar)

These are commonly known as the **Six Articles of Faith** in Sunni theology.

---

# The Five Pillars of Islam

## 1. Shahada (Faith)

Declaration of faith:

> There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.

---

## 2. Salah (Prayer)

- Five daily prayers
- Facing the Kaaba in Mecca
- Performed at prescribed times

---

## 3. Zakat (Charity)

- Obligatory charitable giving for eligible Muslims
- Intended to support those in need and promote social welfare

---

## 4. Sawm (Fasting)

- Observed during the month of Ramadan
- Fasting from dawn until sunset
- Emphasizes self-discipline, worship, and empathy

---

## 5. Hajj (Pilgrimage)

- Pilgrimage to Mecca
- Required once in a lifetime for those who are physically and financially able

---

# Sacred Texts

## Qur'an

The Qur'an is considered by Muslims to be the literal word of God revealed to Prophet Muhammad.

Features:

- 114 chapters (Surahs)
- Written in Arabic
- Recited during worship
- Central source of Islamic teachings

---

## Hadith

Hadith are reports about the sayings, actions, and approvals of Prophet Muhammad.

They are an important source for understanding Islamic practice alongside the Qur'an.

---

# Major Prophets in Islam

Muslims believe God sent many prophets throughout history, including:

- Adam
- Noah (Nuh)
- Abraham (Ibrahim)
- Moses (Musa)
- David (Dawud)
- Solomon (Sulayman)
- Jonah (Yunus)
- John the Baptist (Yahya)
- Jesus (Isa)
- Muhammad (final prophet in Islam)

---

# Allah

In Islam:

- Allah is the one and only God.
- Allah has no partners or equals.
- Allah is eternal and all-powerful.
- Allah is compassionate and merciful.

---

# Angels

Important angels include:

- Jibril (Gabriel) – Revelation
- Mikail (Michael) – Provision
- Israfil – Trumpet before the Day of Judgment
- Malak al-Mawt (Angel of Death)

---

# Important Places

## Mecca

- Birthplace of Prophet Muhammad
- Home of the Kaaba
- Holiest city in Islam

---

## Medina

- City where Muhammad established the first Muslim community
- Contains the Prophet's Mosque

---

## Jerusalem

- Associated with the Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj)
- Home to Al-Aqsa Mosque

---

# Worship

Muslim worship includes:

- Daily prayer
- Reading the Qur'an
- Charity
- Fasting
- Supplication (Dua)
- Remembrance of God (Dhikr)

---

# Major Festivals

## Eid al-Fitr

Celebrated after the end of Ramadan.

---

## Eid al-Adha

Commemorates the willingness of Ibrahim (Abraham) to obey God.

Observed during the Hajj season.

---

# Islamic Calendar

Islam uses a lunar calendar.

Important months include:

- Ramadan
- Shawwal
- Dhu al-Hijjah
- Muharram

---

# Branches of Islam

## Sunni

The largest branch of Islam.

---

## Shia

The second-largest branch, with distinct historical and theological traditions.

---

## Ibadi

A smaller branch primarily found in Oman and some other regions.

# Islamic Law

**Sharia** refers to the broad framework of Islamic moral and legal guidance derived from sources such as the Qur'an and Sunnah. Its interpretation and application vary across schools of thought, countries, and historical contexts.

# Summary

Islam teaches belief in one God, guidance through prophets, ethical living, worship, charity, and accountability in the afterlife. Its teachings are based primarily on the Qur'an and the example of Prophet Muhammad, and it has had a profound influence on the history, culture, philosophy, science, and societies of many regions around the world.
