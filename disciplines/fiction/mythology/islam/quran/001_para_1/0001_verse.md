# Quran 1:1

## Basic Information

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Surah | Al-Fatihah (The Opening) |
| Surah Number | 1 |
| Verse | 1 |
| Arabic | بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ |
| Transliteration | **Bismi llāhi r-raḥmāni r-raḥīm** |
| Common English Translation | "In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful." |

> **Note:** Some Muslim traditions count the opening phrase (*Bismillah*) as the first verse of Surah Al-Fatihah, while others number the verses differently in certain contexts. The numbering above follows the most widely used convention.

# English Translation

Several well-known English translations include:

- **Sahih International:** "In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful."
- **Abdel Haleem:** "In the name of God, the Lord of Mercy, the Giver of Mercy."
- **Pickthall:** "In the name of Allah, the Beneficent, the Merciful."
- **Yusuf Ali:** "In the name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful."

Different translators choose different English words to convey the meanings of the Arabic names of God.

# Context

This verse begins **Surah Al-Fatihah**, the opening chapter of the Quran.

The phrase **"Bismillah"** ("In the name of Allah") also appears at the beginning of almost every surah in the Quran (the primary exception is Surah 9, At-Tawbah). Muslims commonly recite it before reading the Quran and before many everyday actions as an expression of seeking God's blessing and guidance.

---

# Key Themes

- Beginning actions in God's name
- Recognition of God's mercy
- Worship and devotion
- Humility
- Dependence on God

---

# Important Terms

## Allah

The Arabic word for God, used by Muslims and also by many Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews.

---

## Ar-Rahman

One of the names of God in Islam, emphasizing God's vast and universal mercy.

---

## Ar-Rahim

Another name of God emphasizing God's continuing mercy and compassion.

---

# Interpretation

Classical and modern Islamic commentators commonly explain this verse as:

- Beginning every important action by remembering God.
- A reminder that God's mercy is central to the Islamic understanding of God.
- An invocation asking for divine blessing before any undertaking.

Interpretations vary in emphasis among scholars and traditions, but these themes are widely shared.

---

# Usage in Daily Life

Many Muslims recite this phrase:

- Before eating
- Before beginning work or study
- Before traveling
- Before prayer
- Before reading the Quran
- Before many daily activities

# Summary

Quran **1:1** opens the Quran with the phrase **"Bismi llāhi r-raḥmāni r-raḥīm"** ("In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful"). It emphasizes beginning with remembrance of God and highlights divine mercy as a foundational theme throughout the Quran and Islamic practice.
