# Ethics

## Overview

**Ethics** is the branch of philosophy that studies **morality**, **right and wrong**, **good and bad**, **justice**, **virtue**, and **human conduct**. It examines how people ought to act, what makes actions morally right or wrong, and what values should guide individuals and societies.

Ethics influences nearly every aspect of life, including personal behavior, law, business, medicine, science, technology, politics, and environmental responsibility.

# Etymology

The word **Ethics** comes from the Greek word **ethos (ἦθος)**, meaning:

- Character
- Habit
- Custom

The Latin word **moralis** gave rise to the English word **morality**.

# Core Concepts

## Morality

Standards of right and wrong behavior.

---

## Values

Principles or ideals that individuals or societies consider important.

Examples:

- Honesty
- Compassion
- Freedom
- Equality
- Respect

---

## Virtue

A moral excellence or positive character trait.

Examples:

- Courage
- Wisdom
- Temperance
- Justice
- Kindness

---

## Duty

A moral obligation to act in certain ways regardless of personal preferences.

---

## Rights

Entitlements or freedoms recognized as morally or legally justified.

Examples:

- Right to life
- Freedom of speech
- Freedom of religion
- Right to privacy

---

## Responsibility

The obligation to be accountable for one's actions and their consequences.

---

# Major Branches of Ethics

## Metaethics

Studies the nature and meaning of morality.

Questions include:

- What is goodness?
- Are moral values objective?
- What does "right" mean?

---

## Normative Ethics

Attempts to determine moral principles that guide behavior.

It asks:

- What should people do?
- What makes an action morally right?

---

## Applied Ethics

Applies ethical principles to practical issues.

Examples include:

- Medical ethics
- Business ethics
- Environmental ethics
- AI ethics
- Bioethics
- Legal ethics

---

# Major Ethical Theories

## Virtue Ethics

Primary Thinker:

- Aristotle

Focus:

Developing good character rather than merely following rules.

Examples of virtues:

- Courage
- Honesty
- Compassion
- Justice

---

## Deontology

Primary Thinker:

- Immanuel Kant

Focus:

Actions are judged by whether they follow moral duties and rules.

Examples:

- Keep promises
- Tell the truth
- Respect persons

---

## Consequentialism

Focus:

The morality of an action depends on its consequences.

---

### Utilitarianism

Major Thinkers:

- Jeremy Bentham
- John Stuart Mill

Principle:

Choose the action that produces the greatest overall good or happiness.

---

## Care Ethics

Emphasizes:

- Relationships
- Empathy
- Compassion
- Care for others

Major contributors include Carol Gilligan and Nel Noddings.

---

## Contractarianism

Focus:

Moral rules arise from agreements among rational individuals for mutual benefit.

Important thinkers include Thomas Hobbes, John Locke, Jean-Jacques Rousseau (in political philosophy), and John Rawls (modern political philosophy).

# Ethics and Law

Although ethics and law often overlap, they are distinct.

| Ethics | Law |
|---------|-----|
| Based on moral principles | Based on legal rules |
| May vary across cultures and philosophies | Established by governments |
| Violations may lead to social criticism | Violations may lead to legal penalties |

Some actions may be:

- Ethical but illegal
- Legal but unethical
- Both ethical and legal
- Neither ethical nor legal

# Summary

Ethics is the philosophical study of morality and human conduct. It seeks to understand what is right and wrong, how people should act, and what values should guide individuals and societies. Through its major branches—metaethics, normative ethics, and applied ethics—and its diverse theories such as virtue ethics, deontology, and consequentialism, ethics provides essential tools for making thoughtful and responsible decisions in personal, professional, and public life.
